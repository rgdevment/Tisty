# The spread and reminders

Priorities sort what matters; the spread decides **when**.

## A river of days

The spread runs as a river of days you scroll through rather than a week you page:

- **Every day is a row**, and the row grows with what it carries — a heavy Friday is tall, a free Tuesday is a line.
- **What you wrote with no day** waits in a tray on the left. Drag it onto a day, or drag it back to take the day away again.
- **Drop one onto a day that already carries two** and Tisty says so, and offers the next day that is free. It never insists.

## The month

A **month** button is there for looking, not for dealing: the same days laid out as a grid. Press a day and you land on it in the river.

Tisty cannot see the calendar you keep somewhere else, so an empty square is only a square with nothing written on it — never a promise that the day is free.

## Repeating tasks

Repeating tasks come back one occurrence at a time, so the archive shows you did it twelve times. It counts what was **owed** rather than only what you closed: a routine reads *26 of 30*, with four dates that have no record.

Marking one done days late offers those missed dates back instead of calling them forgotten: tick the ones you did, and the gap closes.

How to write a repeat — `every tuesday`, `every 3 days`, `until september 30` — is in [Natural language](Natural-Language#repeating).

## Reminders

A reminder is an hour you choose, and it rings **on this machine and nowhere else**, even with Tisty closed: a system notification and a short sound you can turn off under **Settings › General › Notices**.

- **With Tisty closed**, the system gives the notice. Each time you open Tisty, and with every reminder you set, change or remove, Tisty books the next month's notices with Windows or macOS, and the system shows each at its hour. No internet and no server.
- **The computer has to be on.** With it off nothing rings, and Windows drops the notice if the computer is still off five minutes after its hour.
- **It rings once.** With Tisty open, the notice is the system's own and the window does not repeat it. If the computer was asleep at that hour, Tisty gives it to you when it wakes.
- **A click on the notice** opens Tisty on that task.
- **What arrives with Tisty closed**, such as a reminder your assistant sets or one from another computer, is booked the next time Tisty opens. That is why, when you set your first reminder, Tisty offers to open when you sign in; change it any time under **Settings › General › Start with the computer**.
- **The sound** is the system's notification sound. Turn the short tone off and the notice comes silently; turn the system notification off and Tisty books nothing.
