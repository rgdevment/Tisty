# The spread and reminders

Priorities sort what matters; the spread decides **when**.

## A river of days

The spread runs as a river of days you scroll through rather than a week you page:

- **Every day is a row**, and the row grows with what it carries — a heavy Friday is tall, a free Tuesday is a line.
- **What you wrote with no day** waits in the **With no day** tray, on the left. Drag it onto a day, or drag it back to take the day away again.
- **Drop one onto a day that already carries two** and Tisty says so, and offers the next day that is free. It never insists.

## The month

The **Month** button is there for looking, not for dealing: the same days laid out as a grid. Press a day and you land on it in the river.

Tisty cannot see the calendar you keep somewhere else, so an empty square is only a square with nothing written on it — never a promise that the day is free.

## Repeating tasks

Repeating tasks come back one occurrence at a time, so the archive shows you did it twelve times. It counts what was **owed** rather than only what you closed: a routine reads *26 of 30*, with four dates that have no record.

Marking one done days late offers those missed dates back instead of calling them forgotten: tick the ones you did, and the gap closes.

How to write a repeat — `every tuesday`, `every 3 days`, `until september 30` — is in [Natural language](Natural-Language#repeating).

## Reminders

A reminder is an hour you choose, and it rings **on your computers and nowhere else**, even with Tisty closed: each computer that syncs your Tisty gives its own notice, and no server and no phone does. It is a system notification and a short sound you can turn off under **Settings › Tasks and reminders › Notices**. To be reminded on your phone too, [save the task to your calendar](#in-your-calendar).

- **With Tisty closed**, the system gives the notice. Each time you open Tisty, and with every reminder you set, change or remove, Tisty books up to a month of notices with Windows or macOS, the nearest first and at most 256 on Windows and 64 on a Mac, and the system shows each at its hour; the rest are booked the next times Tisty is open. No internet and no server.
- **The system has to let Tisty notify.** On a Mac, the first reminder makes macOS ask whether Tisty may send notifications. If you refuse there, or turn Tisty's notifications off in the system's own settings, nothing is booked and nothing rings with Tisty closed.
- **The computer has to be on.** With it off nothing rings, and Windows drops the notice if the computer is still off five minutes after its hour.
- **It rings once.** With Tisty open, the notice is the system's own and the window does not repeat it. If the computer was asleep at that hour with Tisty open, Tisty gives it to you when it wakes.
- **A click on the notice** opens Tisty on that task.
- **What arrives with Tisty closed**, such as a reminder your assistant sets or one from another computer, is booked the next time Tisty opens. That is why, the first time you have a reminder and Tisty does not already start with the computer, it offers to open when you sign in; change it any time under **Settings › Look and window › Start with the computer**. If Windows took Tisty's startup away, from Task Manager or from Settings → Apps → Startup, Tisty does not offer it, and that row turns grey with the **Held by the system** note and **Open Windows Settings**, to turn it back on there.
- **The sound** is the system's notification sound. Turn the short tone off and the notice comes silently; turn the system notification off and Tisty books nothing.
- **Important reminders**, off from the start under **Settings › Tasks and reminders › Notices**: with it on, a reminder may come through «Do not disturb» once you allow it in the system. Tisty changes nothing in the system on its own: the row carries the **Held by the system** note with the step, and **Open the system settings** opens that screen. With **A notification from the system** off, the switch turns grey and the **Missing** note offers **Turn it on** first.
  - **Windows 11** (build 22546 or newer): under Settings › System › Notifications › Tisty, allow important notifications. Windows may also ask you in a notice the first time; allow it there too.
  - **macOS**: under System Settings › Notifications › Tisty, keep time-sensitive notifications on, and in Focus allow time-sensitive notifications in each focus you use.
  - If the option shows as unavailable, this computer or this copy of Tisty cannot do it. Tisty does not use **critical alerts**, the ones that ring even on silent: Apple keeps them for health, safety and emergency apps.

## In your calendar

A task with a date goes to your calendar with one button, and from there your calendar reminds you, on your phone too.

- **Choose your calendar once on each computer**, under **Settings › Tasks and reminders › Your calendar**: Google Calendar, Outlook.com or Hotmail, Outlook for work or school, or Apple Calendar. If you choose none, Tisty asks you which one every time, and keeps no answer.
- **On the task, press Save to my calendar.** With Google and Outlook, your browser opens the event already filled in: the title, the day and the hour, and the task's link in Tisty. You press **Save** there. The notice is the one your calendar gives every event.
- **Apple Calendar**, on a Mac only, opens the event in the Calendar app with the task's reminders. If the task has none, it reminds you half an hour before, or the evening before for a whole day, as long as that time has not passed.
- **Which date it takes:** the task's day, or its deadline when it has no day. With an hour, it becomes a half-hour event, ending at the deadline's hour when that is all the task has; without one, a whole-day event. A repeating task saves only its next time.
- **No accounts, no keys:** Tisty does not connect to your calendar and only remembers the one you choose in Settings.

> [!NOTE]
> **The event does not follow the task.** If you change the date in Tisty later, the event stays where it was: move it yourself in your calendar.
