# The archive

**A completed task is not finished. It is archived.**

It stops being a reminder of what to do and becomes the record of how something got solved. This is the reason Tisty exists: months later you search for how you solved something, and it is there — its page with what you wrote along the way, how it went, the steps, the documents you leaned on. The page is put away with the task: it reads but takes no more writing, and comes back if you reopen it.

![Finishing keeps everything: what you close stays, with its steps, journal and files](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rgdevment/Tisty/main/assets/store/en-01-record.png)

## Completing, dropping, erasing

- **Completing** a task marks it done and sends it to the archive, with its steps, notes, attachments and documents intact.
- **Dropping** puts it aside without doing it: it lands in the archive too.
- What is in the archive stays there in case you look for it.

## Three layers

| Layer | What it holds |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Stories** | Tasks that taught something, with their whole trail — what changed, when, and what you wrote along the way |
| **Routines** | Repeating tasks, with their tallies, their streaks and the hour you usually keep them |
| **Trace** | What closed leaving next to nothing written — listed dense and out of the way, because it still happened and search still reaches it |

On its own, a task is a story when you came back to add to it — steps, notes, sections — at more than three separate times; a list you wrote at once and only ticked stays in the trace. You decide which layer each one reads in: in the detail of a closed task, **Move to the trace** or **Move to the stories**. The trail keeps each move.

## Search is the way in

Search is the main way into the archive, not a side feature. Search for a word you remember and it comes up — tasks and documents alike — even if you closed it two years ago. If the word is on a task's page, the task comes up.

The archive is history you consult. A closed task comes back whole: its page with what you read, tried and ruled out, the files on it, its parts and every step, as they were left. A year after a spike like «Find out how to sync with OneDrive», asking «how did we solve the OneDrive uploads?» finds it — and from it, «Upload large files» and «Resume a cut upload» with their own steps and notes. Why a task keeps all that, and when something belongs in a document instead, is in [A task or a document?](Tasks#a-task-or-a-document).

## Erasing for good

> [!CAUTION]
> Erasing is only possible for what is **already closed and reads as a trace**. A story is only ever hidden: to erase it, move it to the trace first. Erasing takes it off this computer, and off your other machines at the next sync, with no undo.

Erasing and hiding are one task at a time, from its detail or its row.

Files you had attached do not go with it, because another document might still use them. They stay behind as loose files, and **Settings › Maintenance** lists them for you to let go of when you want.

> [!NOTE]
> Tisty's log only ever grows. Erasing a task removes it from what you see, not from the history underneath. Keep passwords and secrets out of tasks — see [[Privacy]].

## Keeping the store tidy

**Settings › Maintenance** looks over the store as soon as you open it, and **Check again** looks once more. It counts what is spare, what is missing and what you can get back, and changes nothing on its own:

- Attachments nothing names any more go to the bin, with thirty days to change your mind. Since 1.33, each one says beside it how it came — added in the window, brought by an assistant, with an imported document, in a parcel or as part of the guide — on which computer and when, so you know what you are letting go of; the same goes for the copies of a file saved twice. One kept before 1.33 says only the computer and the day.
- Documents on disk that the log does not name are set apart for you to look at first, because they may be the only copy of something — taking one in can never be wrong.
