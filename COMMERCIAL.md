# Commercial licensing

Tisty is dual licensed.

- **AGPL-3.0** — free for everyone, forever. See [LICENSE](LICENSE).
- **Commercial licence** — for organisations that cannot accept the AGPL's
  terms. Contact <github@apirest.cl>.

Same software, same features. The only thing you buy is a different set of
obligations.

## Do you actually need one?

Almost certainly not. The AGPL asks something of you when you **distribute**
the software or a work derived from it, or when you **modify** it and let
other people interact with your modified version **over a network**. Running
it does not trigger anything, no matter how many people use it or for how
long.

**You do not need a commercial licence to:**

- Install and use Tisty at home or at work, on any number of machines.
- Deploy it across an entire organisation, including internal IT rollouts.
- Read, audit, or fork the source.
- Modify it for your own use, without publishing those changes, as long as
  nobody interacts with your modified version over a network.
- Run `tisty serve` unmodified on your own network.
- Write plugins for it, publish them, and license them however you like — as
  long as they are separate works and not derived from Tisty's own code.
- Contribute changes back.

## When you do need one

The AGPL's obligations kick in at two moments. If either applies and you
cannot meet them, that is what the commercial licence is for.

**When you distribute.** If you ship Tisty, or anything derived from it, to
someone else — as a product, bundled inside a product, or as a modified
build — you must give recipients the complete corresponding source under the
AGPL-3.0.

**When you modify and serve over a network.** This is what separates the AGPL
from the plain GPL. If you change Tisty's code and let users interact with
your modified version remotely — an internal web service, a hosted product,
anything reachable over a network — you must offer those users the source of
your modified version. Not publishing binaries is not a way around it.

In practice, you need a commercial licence when you want to:

- Ship Tisty, or a derivative, inside a closed-source product.
- Run a modified Tisty as a hosted or internal network service without
  releasing your modifications.
- Redistribute under terms other than the AGPL-3.0.
- Meet a procurement policy that rules out copyleft licences outright.

If you are unsure whether your situation triggers any of this, ask before
assuming. A short email is cheaper than a wrong guess in either direction.

## What a commercial licence gives you

- The same code, without the AGPL's copyleft and network obligations.
- The right to distribute and to modify without publishing your changes.
- Written terms your legal department can actually sign.

It does **not** buy exclusive features, early access, or a different build.
There is one Tisty and everyone gets all of it.

## Pricing

Fixed price per tier, billed annually. **Never a percentage of your revenue** —
that model is unauditable for a project this size and gets a product vetoed by
legal departments on sight.

Tiers, current terms and quotes: <github@apirest.cl>.

## What is never sold

These stay free under the AGPL-3.0, permanently, and no commercial tier will
ever gate them:

- The command line interface.
- The complete task module.
- The documents module.
- Synchronisation against your own repository or folder.

If the free product were not complete and dignified on its own, there would be
no project.
